Calculate density from mass and volume

Density formula

Density = Mass ÷ Volume, often written as ρ = m/V. If mass and volume are known, divide the mass by the volume to find density.

Worked example

If a sample has a mass of 100 g and a volume of 20 cm³, its density is 100 ÷ 20 = 5 g/cm³.

How to calculate density

  1. Measure or identify the object's mass.
  2. Measure or identify its volume.
  3. Make sure the mass and volume units are compatible.
  4. Divide mass by volume.
  5. Report the result with the appropriate density unit.

Density units

Mass and volume units must be consistent. Common combinations include g/cm³, kg/m³ and g/mL. Since 1 mL equals 1 cm³, g/mL and g/cm³ are numerically equivalent when those units are used together.

What density tells you

Density describes how much mass is contained in a given volume. A higher density means more mass is packed into the same amount of space, while a lower density means less mass is present for that volume.

Common uses of a density calculator

Density calculations are used in school science, laboratory work, engineering, material comparison and basic physics problems when mass and volume are known.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing incompatible mass and volume units without conversion.
  • Using zero volume, which cannot be used as a divisor.
  • Leaving the density unit out of the final answer.
  • Assuming this basic formula automatically accounts for temperature, pressure or composition effects.

Important limitation

This calculator performs the basic density relationship. It does not automatically convert incompatible units or account for changes in density caused by temperature, pressure or composition.

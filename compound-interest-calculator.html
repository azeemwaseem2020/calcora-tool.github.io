THE BIG IDEA

Time can change the outcome

With compounding, previously earned interest is added to the balance and can earn interest too. That is why the same rate can produce very different results over different time periods.

Keep in mind: this simple calculation does not include taxes, fees, inflation, withdrawals or future deposits.

How compound interest works

The standard formula is A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt). P is the starting amount, r is the annual rate written as a decimal, n is the number of compounding periods per year and t is time in years.

For example, increasing the compounding frequency while keeping the rate and time unchanged can slightly increase the final value. Real savings and investment products may use different rules, so check the account terms before relying on an estimate.

5KW SOLAR SYSTEM GUIDE

5kW Solar System Calculator: Panels, Inverter, Battery & Roof Area

A 5kW label tells you the nominal PV or inverter capacity; it does not automatically tell you the correct panel count, battery size or expected bill savings.

How many panels make 5kW?

The simple panel count is target watts divided by panel watts, rounded up. With 550W modules, 5,000 ÷ 550 = 9.09, so 10 panels would provide about 5.5 kW of nominal panel capacity. A real array may be designed differently to match the inverter's permitted DC input, string voltage and the module's electrical characteristics.

How much energy can 5kW produce?

Production depends on the available sun and system losses. A first-pass estimate is 5 kW × peak sun hours × system efficiency per day. For example, at 5 peak sun hours and 80% overall efficiency, 5 × 5 × 0.8 = about 20 kWh per day. This is an estimate, not a guaranteed output; season, heat, dust, shading, orientation and equipment can change the result.

Does a 5kW system need a 5kW battery?

No. PV or inverter capacity and battery energy are different measurements. Battery size should follow the energy you want stored and the hours of backup you need. A home that backs up only lights, fans, a refrigerator and internet may need much less storage than a system intended to run the whole property.

Inverter selection

The inverter must suit the intended AC load as well as the PV array. Check continuous output, surge capability, PV voltage and current limits, MPPT range, battery compatibility and grid requirements. Do not assume that a 5kW PV array automatically requires an identical inverter model or rating.

Roof area

Estimate panel area from panel count × physical panel area, then allow additional room for mounting, access, setbacks and obstructions. For example, if 10 panels each occupy 2.3 m², the panel faces alone occupy about 23 m²; the actual usable roof area will normally need to be larger.

What determines whether 5kW is suitable?

Energy use

Review recent electricity consumption in kWh rather than choosing a system from appliance wattage alone.

Daytime demand

Consider how much electricity is used while solar production is available.

Backup goal

Decide which loads must run during outages before selecting battery storage and inverter capacity.

Planning for Pakistan

For a Pakistan installation, local solar conditions, summer heat, dust, roof orientation, shading, electricity usage and the selected equipment all affect results. Grid-connected and backup systems also have different design requirements. Get the final electrical design and installation checked against the applicable local requirements and manufacturer specifications.

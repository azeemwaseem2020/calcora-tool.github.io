Fertilizer Calculator

Plan a fertilizer application from a known recommendation, build a multi-product blend, check N-P-K-S supply against nutrient targets, calculate bags and estimate purchase cost.

1. Field and units

Use the same area basis as your agronomic recommendation. The calculator does not silently convert a rate from kg/acre to kg/ha.

2. Products in the plan

Add up to four products. Enter the analysis printed on the actual fertilizer label. Examples: Urea 46-0-0, DAP 18-46-0, MOP 0-0-60. Rates are product kg per selected area unit.

3. Optional nutrient targets

Enter target nutrient rates only when you already have a crop/soil recommendation. These fields are a comparison check, not a recommendation engine.

FIELD-READY WORKFLOW

More than a bag calculator

Calcora combines the most useful parts of professional fertilizer planning: field area, product analysis, multiple products, nutrient totals, bag quantities, cost and a target check.

Important: this tool calculates from inputs you provide. It does not diagnose soil or invent a crop nutrient recommendation. Use a current soil test, crop-specific recommendation, product label and appropriate local agronomic guidance.

Why this fertilizer calculator is different

A simple fertilizer calculator tells you how many bags are needed. A better planning tool also shows what those products actually supply, lets you combine several products, compares the result with a known nutrient target and separates mathematical quantities from agronomic recommendations. This version is designed around that workflow.

How the calculation works

Total product per field = application rate × field area. Nutrient supplied per selected area = product rate × nutrient percentage ÷ 100. For multiple products, Calcora adds the nutrient contribution from every product before comparing it with any target you enter.

Example: urea, DAP and MOP

Suppose a plan uses 100 kg/ha of urea 46-0-0, 100 kg/ha of DAP 18-46-0 and 50 kg/ha of MOP 0-0-60. The calculator keeps each product separate, then totals their N, P₂O₅ and K₂O supply. This makes the nitrogen contribution from DAP visible instead of accidentally treating all nitrogen as coming from urea.

Urea calculator

For a product labelled 46-0-0, every 100 kg of product contains 46 kg N by label analysis. If the rate is 50 kg/acre, the mathematical N supply is 23 kg/acre. Always use the analysis printed on the product you are actually purchasing.

DAP calculator

A DAP product commonly supplies both N and P₂O₅. A label such as 18-46-0 means 18% N and 46% P₂O₅ by weight. If DAP is part of a larger plan, its nitrogen contribution should be counted before deciding whether another N source is needed. citeturn0search5

MOP / potash calculator

A product labelled 0-0-60 supplies K₂O but no N or P₂O₅ under that label analysis. Enter the actual grade rather than assuming every potash product has the same analysis.

Acres versus hectares

A rate of 50 kg/acre is not the same field application rate as 50 kg/ha because an acre and a hectare have different areas. The safest workflow is to keep the recommendation and field unit aligned before doing any multiplication.

Bag calculation

Exact bags = total product ÷ bag size. If you must buy whole bags, the practical purchase quantity is rounded up. Calcora reports both the exact mathematical equivalent and whole bags so you can distinguish field-rate arithmetic from purchasing logistics.

Fertilizer cost

Enter a product price per bag to estimate cost. The tool calculates product-specific cost and total plan cost. Real farm cost can also include delivery, handling, application and other charges, so the displayed value should be treated as a purchase estimate rather than a complete farm budget.

Target nutrient check

If you already have a recommendation such as 120 kg N/ha, enter it as a target. Calcora compares the calculated supply with that target and reports the difference. A shortfall means the products entered do not mathematically supply the target; a surplus means they supply more than the target. Neither result is permission to change a recommendation without agronomic review.

Soil testing and fertilizer recommendations

Fertilizer rates should be based on appropriate crop, soil and local conditions. Extension guidance describes using soil-test information and crop recommendations to determine actual nutrient requirements, then converting the required amount of nutrient into fertilizer product using the nutrient fraction on the label. citeturn0search5

What this calculator does not do

  • It does not diagnose nutrient deficiencies.
  • It does not create a universal crop recommendation.
  • It does not replace a current soil test or local agronomist.
  • It does not guarantee yield or profit.
  • It does not assume a fertilizer grade when you provide a different label analysis.

Practical fertilizer planning checklist

1. Confirm the field

Check actual acreage/hectares and keep the unit consistent.

2. Check the recommendation

Use a current crop and soil-based recommendation.

3. Read the label

Enter the actual N-P-K-S analysis printed on the bag.

4. Check nutrient supply

Compare product-derived nutrients with the known target.

5. Plan purchases

Review exact kg, whole bags and product-specific cost.

6. Review locally

Confirm application timing, compatibility and rates with appropriate local guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Can I calculate fertilizer for an acre?

Yes. Select Acres and enter the rate in kg/acre. Keep the rate basis consistent with your recommendation.

Can I calculate fertilizer for a hectare?

Yes. Select Hectares and enter the rate in kg/ha.

Can I add urea and DAP together?

Yes. Add each as a separate product with its own rate and label analysis. Calcora will total their nutrient contributions.

Can this calculator choose the best fertilizer automatically?

No. Product selection depends on crop needs, soil test results, available products, price, application strategy and local agronomic advice. Calcora keeps that decision separate from the arithmetic.

Does the calculator support custom fertilizer grades?

Yes. You can enter the N, P₂O₅, K₂O and S percentages printed on your product label.

Does it calculate bags?

Yes. Enter the bag size and the tool reports exact bag-equivalent quantity and whole bags to purchase for each product.

Safety and accuracy note

Fertilizer decisions can affect crop performance, farm economics and nutrient losses. Verify important rates with current soil information, crop-specific recommendations, the actual product label and qualified local agricultural guidance before application.

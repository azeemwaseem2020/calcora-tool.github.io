What this TMR calculator answers

It addresses practical quantity questions: how much of each TMR ingredient is needed, how many kilograms go into a batch, how much feed a group uses each day and how long a prepared batch may cover that group.

TMR ingredient quantity formula

Ingredient weight = batch weight × ingredient percentage ÷ 100. For example, 250 kg from a 1,000 kg batch at 25% is 250 kg. The same arithmetic is repeated for every ingredient entered.

500 kg, 1,000 kg and 2,000 kg TMR batches

For 25% of the mix, a 500 kg batch contains 125 kg, a 1,000 kg batch contains 250 kg and a 2,000 kg batch contains 500 kg. The calculator can be used with any practical batch size while keeping the formulation percentages on the same basis.

Worked TMR batch example

Suppose a 1,000 kg batch contains 50% forage, 30% concentrate and 20% other ingredients. The calculator returns 500 kg forage, 300 kg concentrate and 200 kg other ingredients. If 20 animals each receive 20 kg/day, the group uses 400 kg/day and the mathematical batch coverage is 2.5 days.

TMR ration calculator: percentage versus kilograms

A ration percentage becomes useful for mixing only after it is tied to a batch weight. If a formulation is 50% forage and the target batch is 1,000 kg, the forage quantity is 500 kg. Always confirm that all ingredient percentages use the same basis.

Dry matter versus as-fed TMR

Feeds can contain very different amounts of water. A percentage supplied on a dry-matter basis is not automatically the same physical loading weight as an as-fed percentage. When the formulation is on a dry-matter basis, use the actual dry-matter content of each ingredient to convert the target into loading weights.

Daily feed use and batch coverage

Group feed use is animals × TMR per animal per day. Batch coverage is batch weight ÷ daily group feed. These outputs are arithmetic checks, not recommendations that a particular feeding rate is appropriate.

TMR calculator for dairy cows and buffalo

Dairy cattle and buffalo can have different requirements depending on body weight, milk production, lactation stage, forage quality, body condition and available feeds. A generic online ration should not replace a properly formulated diet.

Why forage quality matters

The same physical quantity of two forages can supply very different nutrients. Moisture, dry matter, fibre, energy, protein and mineral content can all affect the nutritional value of the final mix. Quantity arithmetic is only one part of feed formulation.

Mixing and scale checks

  • Confirm the mixer can safely handle the planned batch.
  • Use current moisture or dry-matter values where the formulation requires them.
  • Check every ingredient weight on the mixer scale.
  • Keep dry-matter and as-fed calculations clearly separated.
  • Recheck the formulation if an ingredient, forage lot or animal group changes.

FAQ: TMR calculator

Does this calculator create a complete dairy ration?

No. It converts a known formulation into quantities and checks the arithmetic. Nutritional formulation needs animal, forage and laboratory information.

Can I calculate a 500 kg or 2,000 kg batch?

Yes. Enter the actual target batch size and keep all ingredient percentages on the same basis.

Can I use it for dry matter?

Yes for arithmetic planning, but dry-matter formulations must be converted using the actual moisture or dry-matter content before loading physical kilograms.

Turn a known ration into a workable batch

Calcora handles the arithmetic: percentages become kilograms and the batch is checked for completeness. It does not decide what a cow should be fed.

Important: a suitable ration depends on animal class, body weight, milk production, forage analysis, dry matter, energy, protein, fibre and minerals. Use a qualified nutritionist or trusted formulation for the actual ration.

What this TMR calculator answers

Common practical questions include: how much of each TMR ingredient is needed, how many kilograms are in a batch, how much feed is used per day and how long a batch may cover a group of animals.

TMR ingredient quantity formula

Ingredient weight = batch weight × ingredient percentage ÷ 100. For example, 250 kg from a 1,000 kg batch at 25% is 250 kg. The calculator repeats that calculation for every ingredient entered.

TMR ration and dry matter

A TMR can contain feeds with very different moisture levels. A percentage supplied on a dry-matter basis cannot simply be treated as the same physical loading weight on an as-fed basis. If a formulation is on a dry-matter basis, use the actual dry-matter percentage of each feed before loading the mixer.

Daily feed and batch coverage

If 20 animals each receive 20 kg of TMR per day, the group uses 400 kg/day. A 1,000 kg batch would cover about 2.5 days. This is a quantity calculation, not a recommendation for the feeding rate.

TMR calculator for dairy cows

Dairy cows at different stages of lactation do not have identical requirements. Body weight, milk yield, forage quality, body condition and available ingredients can change a suitable ration. A generic online recipe should not be treated as a complete nutrition plan.

Mixing and scale checks

  • Confirm the mixer can safely handle the planned batch.
  • Use actual moisture or dry-matter values when the formulation requires them.
  • Check each ingredient weight on the mixer scale.
  • Keep the same basis—dry matter or as-fed—throughout the calculation.
  • Recheck the formulation if an ingredient, forage lot or animal group changes.

Solar Battery Backup Calculator: kWh, Ah & Backup Hours

Battery sizing becomes much clearer when you separate the load you want backed up from the total appliances in the building.

Basic relationship: required usable battery energy ≈ backup load (kW) × backup hours. Nominal battery capacity must then account for usable depth of discharge and conversion losses.

Example

If essential backup loads average 600 W and you want 5 hours, usable energy is about 3 kWh. A battery bank must be larger than 3 kWh if only part of its stored energy is intended to be used or if conversion losses are significant.

Ah versus kWh

kWh describes stored energy. Ah describes charge capacity at a stated voltage. A simplified conversion is Ah ≈ kWh × 1,000 ÷ volts. Always compare batteries at the same voltage and understand whether a quoted capacity is nominal or usable.

Do not size the battery from connected load alone

A home may have a 5 kW connected load but only need lights, fans, refrigerator and internet during an outage. Entering the entire connected load can make the battery unnecessarily large. For accurate planning, define the essential backup circuit or backup wattage.

Battery chemistry and real-world limits

Lithium and lead-acid batteries have different usable capacities, charge limits, cycle behavior, temperature requirements and costs. Inverter efficiency, battery temperature, cable losses and manufacturer limits also affect runtime. This calculator is a planning aid, not a battery warranty or engineering design.

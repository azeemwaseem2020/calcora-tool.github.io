Why four inputs matter

Daily kWh tells us what the system must produce. Sun hours and efficiency translate that demand into PV capacity. Panel wattage turns capacity into a practical module count.

Do not size from appliance wattage alone if you already have a measured monthly energy bill or daily kWh estimate.

The formula behind the estimate

PV capacity (kW) = daily energy (kWh) ÷ [peak sun hours × system efficiency]. Panel count is the required capacity in watts divided by panel wattage, rounded up because a real system needs a whole number of panels.

Worked example

For 15 kWh/day, 5 peak sun hours and 80% efficiency, the first-pass requirement is 3.75 kW. With 550 W modules, 6.82 panels are required mathematically, so the practical count rounds up to 7 panels, giving 3.85 kW of nameplate capacity.

Pakistan solar planning: what can change the answer?

  • Seasonal sunlight and weather
  • Roof orientation, tilt and shading
  • Panel temperature and dust
  • DC/AC wiring and inverter losses
  • Daytime self-consumption versus battery charging
  • Inverter maximum PV input and string-voltage limits

Common mistake

A 5 kW panel array does not mean it will deliver 5 kW continuously. Rated PV capacity is a nameplate value under test conditions; real output varies throughout the day.

FAQ

Can I use a different panel wattage?

Yes. Enter the exact rated wattage you are comparing and the calculator will recalculate the module count.

Should I add extra panels?

Potentially, but oversizing should be checked against inverter limits, roof space, electrical design and the intended energy-storage strategy.

Loan EMI: What Your Monthly Payment Really Tells You

A monthly EMI is useful, but it is only one part of the cost of borrowing.

Quick takeaway: compare the monthly payment with the total repayment and total interest. A comfortable EMI can still represent a costly loan over a long term.

What is EMI?

Equated Monthly Instalment, commonly called EMI, is the scheduled payment used for many reducing-balance loans. The payment is influenced by the amount borrowed, the interest rate and the repayment period.

Why the loan term matters

A longer term can make each monthly payment easier to manage, but it usually gives interest more time to accumulate. A shorter term often increases the monthly payment while reducing the number of months over which interest is charged.

Three numbers worth checking

Monthly EMI

The recurring amount used in your monthly budget.

Total interest

The interest portion paid across the scheduled term.

Total repayment

Principal plus calculated interest over the loan term.

Before making a decision

Check the lender's actual schedule for fees, taxes, insurance, variable rates, early-repayment conditions and other charges. Those items may not be part of a basic EMI formula.

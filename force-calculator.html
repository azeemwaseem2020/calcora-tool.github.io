Calculate force from mass and acceleration

Force formula

F = m × a, where F is force in newtons (N), m is mass in kilograms (kg), and a is acceleration in metres per second squared (m/s²).

How to calculate force

  1. Enter the mass in kilograms.
  2. Enter the acceleration in m/s².
  3. Multiply mass by acceleration.
  4. Report the result in newtons.

Worked example

For a 10 kg object accelerating at 2 m/s², F = 10 × 2 = 20 N.

Understanding newtons

One newton is the force required to accelerate a 1 kg mass at 1 m/s². The unit follows directly from the relationship N = kg·m/s².

Mass, acceleration and force

For a fixed acceleration, increasing mass increases the required net force proportionally. For a fixed mass, increasing acceleration also increases force proportionally.

Common mistakes

  • Using grams instead of kilograms without converting the mass.
  • Using speed or velocity where acceleration is required.
  • Mixing incompatible units.
  • Confusing net force with an individual force when several forces act on an object.

When to use this calculator

This calculator is useful for introductory physics, mechanics, classroom exercises and simple motion problems where mass and acceleration are known.

Important limitation

This is a basic Newtonian calculation of net force from mass and acceleration. It does not model friction, drag, changing mass, relativistic effects or complex systems with multiple forces.

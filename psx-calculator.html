What this advanced PSX calculator does

This tool is designed around common Pakistan Stock Exchange search intent: PSX calculator, PSX profit calculator, share profit and loss calculator Pakistan, break-even share price, stock return calculator, dividend calculator and dividend yield calculator.

PSX profit and loss formula

Invested value = average buy price × shares. Sale value = current or sale price × shares. Gross profit/loss = sale value − invested value. Net profit/loss = gross profit/loss − buy-side costs − sell-side costs − optional estimated CGT.

Break-even price

Break-even = (invested value + buy-side costs + sell-side costs) ÷ shares. If an estimated CGT rate is entered, the displayed tax estimate is based only on the positive gain after entered trading costs.

Annualized return

When a holding period is entered, Calcora estimates annualized return from the net return and elapsed days. Annualization can be misleading for very short holding periods, so treat it as a comparison metric rather than a forecast.

Dividend income and yield

Dividend income = dividend per share × shares. Dividend yield = dividend per share ÷ reference share price × 100. Dividend is an important PSX investor metric; PSX explains that dividend yield measures cash dividend relative to share price. citeturn0search2

Brokerage commission, taxes and other transaction charges can materially change the amount you keep. PSX states that commissions can vary between brokerage firms and advises investors to consider applicable taxes and charges. citeturn0search4

Example

Suppose you buy 1,000 shares at PKR 100 and sell at PKR 120. Your invested value is PKR 100,000 and sale value is PKR 120,000, giving PKR 20,000 gross profit before costs and tax. Enter your actual charges to turn that into a more useful net estimate.

Before using the result

  • Use the actual average cost from your trade history.
  • Enter buy and sell costs separately when known.
  • Use an applicable tax rate only when you know which rule applies to your transaction.
  • Check corporate actions such as dividends, rights and bonus issues.
  • Reconcile the estimate with your broker statement.
  • Do not treat calculator output as a recommendation to buy or sell.

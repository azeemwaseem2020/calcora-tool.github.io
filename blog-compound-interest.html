Compound Interest Calculator Guide: Formula, Growth and Examples

Compounding is simple to describe: returns can become part of the balance and then earn returns themselves.

Quick answer: the result depends mainly on the starting amount, interest rate, time, and how often interest is compounded.

What is compound interest?

With simple interest, interest is calculated on the original principal. With compound interest, accumulated interest is added to the balance, so later periods can earn interest on a larger amount.

Compound interest formula

For a standard fixed-rate calculation with no extra deposits or withdrawals, the common formula is:

A = P(1 + r/n)nt

P is the starting principal, r is the annual rate written as a decimal, n is the number of compounding periods per year, and t is time in years. The interest earned is A − P.

What changes the result?

Starting amount

A larger principal creates a larger base for future growth.

Interest rate

A higher assumed rate increases the projected future value.

Time

More years give compounding more opportunities to affect the balance.

Why compounding frequency matters

Monthly, quarterly and annual compounding can produce different results even when the quoted annual rate looks the same. Always check whether a rate is nominal, effective, fixed or variable before comparing products.

A simple example

Suppose you start with 1,000 units of currency, use a fixed annual rate, and leave the balance untouched for several years. The calculator applies the chosen compounding frequency to show the projected ending balance. Changing the rate or time lets you see how sensitive the result is.

What a calculator estimate leaves out

Real outcomes can be affected by taxes, fees, inflation, withdrawals, additional deposits, changing rates and investment performance. Treat a compound interest result as a scenario, not a guaranteed return.

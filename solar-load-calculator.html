How the solar calculator works

When monthly electricity units are known, the calculator converts monthly energy into an average daily requirement and estimates PV capacity using peak sun hours and an assumed system efficiency. If you use appliances instead, it adds watt-hours for each appliance to estimate daily energy.

Daily appliance energy: watts × hours per day ÷ 1,000. First-pass PV size: daily kWh ÷ (peak sun hours × efficiency). Panel count is then rounded up using the selected panel wattage.

Why this is better than a panel-count-only calculator

Panel count alone does not describe a complete solar system. The load profile affects inverter selection, while backup hours and the actual backed-up load affect battery sizing. Roof area, shading, orientation and equipment specifications also matter. Professional PV models can include detailed location and loss assumptions; NREL's PVWatts documentation, for example, models multiple system-loss categories rather than assuming all panel nameplate power reaches the AC side.

Solar panel calculator: how many panels do you need?

As a simple example, a 5 kW target with 550 W panels requires 5,000 ÷ 550 = 9.09, so the arithmetic rounds up to 10 panels, giving 5.5 kW of installed nameplate capacity. The correct final panel count depends on the desired energy production, module specifications, inverter limits, roof layout and site conditions.

Solar battery calculator

Battery planning should start with the load you actually want during an outage. A rough relationship is backup load (kW) × backup hours for usable energy. Dividing by usable depth of discharge gives a rough nominal battery-energy requirement. Battery voltage converts energy into an approximate Ah figure. Battery round-trip efficiency, temperature, battery chemistry, aging and manufacturer limits can change the final specification.

Solar inverter size calculator

Inverter sizing should consider continuous load, simultaneous loads and surge requirements. Refrigerators, pumps, motors and compressors can have starting characteristics that a simple connected-watt calculation does not capture. PV DC input limits, MPPT voltage/current range, battery compatibility and grid requirements also need to be checked against the selected inverter.

Solar calculator for Pakistan

For Pakistan, electricity units from the latest bill are generally more useful than guessing consumption from the rupee bill alone. Tariffs, taxes, adjustments and solar export arrangements can change, so Calcora does not hard-code a supposedly universal electricity price into this sizing calculator. If you need a bill-and-savings model, use the units on your bill and verify current utility and regulatory rules before making a purchase decision.

5kW solar system calculator

A 5 kW system is a useful example, not a universal recommendation. With 550 W modules, the nameplate arithmetic gives 10 panels = 5.5 kW. Expected energy depends on solar resource and losses, while inverter and battery requirements depend on the actual load and backup plan.

Common mistakes when sizing solar

  • Using bill amount instead of monthly units when units are available.
  • Assuming every daylight hour is a peak-sun hour.
  • Choosing an inverter only from total panel wattage.
  • Sizing a battery from total connected load when only selected circuits need backup.
  • Ignoring shading, roof orientation and temperature.
  • Treating a calculator result as a final electrical design.

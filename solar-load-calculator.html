How solar system sizing works

A useful first step is to calculate daily energy consumption. For each appliance, energy use is approximately wattage × hours per day ÷ 1,000. Adding the loads gives daily kWh.

A simple PV capacity estimate is daily energy ÷ (peak sun hours × assumed system efficiency). This is an early-stage sizing method. Professional models can account for location-specific weather, module and inverter characteristics, temperature, shading and other losses. Solar panels, inverter and battery

Solar panels produce DC electricity. An inverter converts DC electricity to AC for typical household loads and grid use. Batteries can store energy for use when sunlight is unavailable. These are separate parts of a complete PV system and must be sized together rather than treating the panel count as the entire system. citeturn0search1turn0search2

What are peak sun hours?

Peak sun hours are a simplified way to represent the equivalent number of hours of full solar irradiance available to a PV system. The value is location- and season-dependent, so it should not be confused with the total number of daylight hours. For higher-confidence estimates, use site-specific solar resource data or a detailed PV model.

Why system efficiency matters

Real PV systems experience losses from factors such as soiling, shading, wiring, mismatch, connections, temperature and equipment performance. PVWatts exposes several loss categories and uses an overall loss model rather than assuming every watt of panel nameplate power becomes usable AC energy. citeturn0search0

How to choose an inverter size

The inverter should be selected from the actual AC load profile and the PV array's electrical characteristics, not simply by multiplying panel count. Surge loads from motors, pumps, refrigerators and other equipment can matter. String voltage, maximum current, MPPT range, battery compatibility and grid requirements also need to match the selected inverter.

Battery sizing basics

A rough battery-energy estimate is backup load in kW × backup hours. To convert required usable energy into nominal battery capacity, account for the chosen usable depth of discharge and other battery-system losses. The battery calculation on this page is therefore an estimate, not a recommendation for a particular battery technology.

Roof area and panel count

Panel count is approximately required PV capacity in watts divided by the selected panel wattage, rounded up. Actual roof space must also allow for mounting, access, setbacks, orientation and shading. The calculator's area figure is only a simple panel-area estimate.

On-grid, hybrid and off-grid systems

An on-grid system normally works with the utility grid and may not provide backup during an outage unless it is specifically designed with suitable storage and inverter functionality. Hybrid systems combine grid, solar and storage. Off-grid systems require enough generation and storage for the intended load and operating conditions. The U.S. Department of Energy notes that ordinary grid-connected solar is generally designed to shut down during grid outages for safety, while properly configured solar-plus-storage can provide backup capability. citeturn0search10

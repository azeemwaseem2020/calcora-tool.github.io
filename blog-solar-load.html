Solar Load Calculator Guide: How to Size a Solar System

Before asking how many panels you need, work out what your home or business actually uses.

Quick answer: calculate appliance watts × daily hours to estimate energy use, convert watts to kWh, then use daily energy, peak sun hours and an efficiency assumption for a first-pass solar size.

Watts and kWh are not the same

Watts describe power at a moment in time. Kilowatt-hours describe energy used over time. For an appliance, a simple estimate is watts × hours ÷ 1,000 = kWh.

Worked example

A 1,000-watt load running for 5 hours uses about 5 kWh. Add the daily energy estimates for your lights, fans, refrigerator, pump, television, computer, air conditioner and other appliances to get a rough daily total.

How a solar load estimate works

A common planning relationship is solar system size (kW) = daily energy demand ÷ (peak sun hours × system efficiency). The efficiency factor represents losses from temperature, inverter conversion, wiring, dust and other system conditions.

Why solar sizing varies

Appliance load

Peak watts affect inverter selection and the loads that can run at the same time.

Daily energy

Hours of use determine how many kWh your system needs to produce.

Sun and losses

Location, season, shading, orientation and system losses affect actual generation.

These phrases can mean slightly different calculations, so start with the exact load and energy information you have.

Solar planning in Pakistan

For homes in Pakistan, electricity consumption, daytime usage, seasonal air-conditioning demand and local solar conditions can all affect the right system size. A calculator is useful for an initial estimate, but a final design should account for site conditions, inverter limits, battery needs, roof orientation, shading and local installation requirements.

What the calculator cannot decide

A first-pass estimate is not a substitute for a site survey. Final system design may require peak-load checks, battery sizing, inverter selection, cable and protection design, roof assessment and local technical requirements.

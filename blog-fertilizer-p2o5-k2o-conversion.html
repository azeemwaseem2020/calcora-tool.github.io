P₂O₅ to P and K₂O to K: Fertilizer Conversion Guide

Fertilizer labels and technical recommendations can use different nutrient expressions. The first step is identifying the basis before doing the arithmetic.

P₂O₅ to elemental P

P ≈ P₂O₅ × 0.44

For example, 100 kg P₂O₅ corresponds to about 44 kg elemental P.

Elemental P to P₂O₅

P₂O₅ ≈ P ÷ 0.44

For example, 44 kg elemental P corresponds to approximately 100 kg P₂O₅.

K₂O to elemental K

K ≈ K₂O × 0.83

For example, 100 kg K₂O corresponds to about 83 kg elemental K.

Elemental K to K₂O

K₂O ≈ K ÷ 0.83

Use the reverse conversion when a recommendation is expressed as elemental potassium but the fertilizer label uses K₂O.

Why this matters

Do not compare a P₂O₅ target directly with an elemental-P target. Convert both to the same basis first. The same rule applies to K₂O and elemental K.

Common mistakes

  • Applying the 0.44 factor twice.
  • Using 0.83 when the target is already K₂O.
  • Mixing nutrient bases inside a blend calculation.
  • Assuming fertilizer labels use elemental P and K.

FAQ

Is P₂O₅ the same as phosphorus?

No. P₂O₅ is the conventional fertilizer-label expression; elemental P is a different mass basis.

Is K₂O the same as potassium?

No. K₂O is the conventional fertilizer-label expression; elemental K is different.

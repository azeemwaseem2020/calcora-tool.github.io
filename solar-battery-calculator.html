Size from the real backup job

Start with the equipment that must remain powered and the hours it must run. Then account for the portion of battery capacity you can actually use.

For refrigerators, pumps and motors, starting/surge demand may be higher than running watts.

Battery sizing formula

Delivered energy = load (kW) × backup hours. A planning estimate for nominal battery energy is delivered energy ÷ [usable depth of discharge × efficiency]. Approximate amp-hours are nominal watt-hours ÷ battery-bank voltage.

Worked example

A 1,000 W load running for 4 hours needs 4.00 kWh of delivered energy. With 80% usable discharge and 90% efficiency, the planning estimate is about 5.56 kWh of nominal battery energy. At 48 V, that is about 116 Ah.

Why the calculator asks for depth of discharge

A battery's advertised energy is not necessarily the same as energy you should routinely draw. Usable capacity depends on battery chemistry, manufacturer recommendations, temperature, age and operating conditions.

Pakistan solar planning checklist

  • Separate essential backup loads from optional loads.
  • Check inverter continuous and surge ratings.
  • Confirm battery voltage compatibility.
  • Use the manufacturer's recommended charge/discharge limits.
  • Allow for temperature, aging and future load changes.
  • Check whether your solar array can recharge the battery within the required time.

FAQ

Can I use this for lithium batteries?

Yes, as a planning estimate. Use the battery manufacturer's specified usable capacity and operating limits for final selection.

What about lead-acid?

Lead-acid systems can have different practical discharge limits and performance under higher loads. Do not assume every battery can deliver its nameplate capacity under every condition.
